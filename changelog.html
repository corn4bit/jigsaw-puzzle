[1.0.0]

First release: a complete jigsaw game with a full casual meta, ready to re-skin and ship.

Puzzle

  • Procedural jigsaw cutting from any picture: any resolution, any aspect ratio, any grid from 2×2 upwards. Finished pictures are seamless.
  • Levels authored as PuzzleLevel assets and ordered in a PuzzleLevelLibrary, with no limit on how many a game can have. Levels can also be built in memory from a texture, so a photo the player picked plays like any other level.
  • Piece tray: a few pieces at a time on a shelf under or beside the board, refilled from a queue that favours edges and pieces that fit what is already placed, with a reroll button. The classic scatter layout is one setting away.
  • A dragged piece follows the finger, floats just above it on touch screens so it is never hidden under the fingertip, joins its neighbours, and is kept on screen.
  • One to three stars per level by solving time, with the best time kept. Solved levels can be replayed for a better time.
  • Hint that rings the most useful piece and its slot in a gold glow until the piece is placed, with a gate (CanShowHint) for charging for hints.
  • Per-level saving that remembers every piece and the tray, through a pluggable storage backend (IPuzzleSaveStore).
  • Landscape and portrait from the same prefab, with safe-area support. Rotating mid-level lays the loose pieces out again, keeping joined groups together.
  • Streak praise: Good!, Great!, Awesome! and more pop over the board with sparks and a chime when pieces land in their slots one after another (Puzzle Combo Praise).
  • The finished picture stays in full view for a moment before the win popup, and the HUD, boosters and tray fade out behind it.
  • Sound effects with volume and mute on one component.
  • Jelly press feedback on every button, and touch targets of at least 48 dp on phones.

Level map

  • A winding road with one node per level showing its picture, number, stars and lock, a ring on the current level and the road coloured up to it. Sideways on wide screens, upwards on tall ones; it scrolls to the current level on its own.
  • Chapters (zones) with their own banner, tint and backdrop picture, repeated along the road at their own proportions and reaching under the HUD and tab bar. The demo ships a meadow backdrop.
  • Only the nodes on screen exist, so a thousand levels scroll as smoothly as ten.
  • Scenery around the road (Puzzle Map Scenery): groves of trees, bushes, flowers, a cottage, a pond and more, placed from a grid so the map always looks the same, never on the road, a node or a chapter banner, in both orientations. Rays of sun slant down from under the leaves of some trees and light warm patches on the grass.
  • A living map: a warm sunlit corner, cloud shadows drifting over the meadow, butterflies and sparkles (Puzzle Map Ambience), all at no cost on long maps.
  • Backdrops that tile by themselves can repeat without mirroring (Mirror Copies), so side-view art such as trees never stands upside down.

Meta game

  • Meta core in its own assembly: currencies and boosters as assets, a wallet and an inventory that never go below zero or past a cap, buying in one step, static events for every change, and saving through the same save store as the levels.
  • Menu shell: five tabs (Shop, Ranks, Home, Friends, Profile), along the bottom on tall screens and down the side on wide ones, features that open at a set level, a top HUD with live currency plaques, a loading screen and a settings window.
  • Coin rewards for solved levels, by stars and doubled on the first clear; coins fly from the win popup into the coin plaque.
  • Hint stock that refills one every 20 minutes in real time, with an out-of-hints offer.
  • Five boosters that work out of the box: Magnet, Frame and Stopwatch in the level, Ready Edge and Double Coins on the level start window. Each opens at a set level, is introduced by a tapping finger the first time, and can be bought on the spot.
  • Shop with a special offer, bundles, gem and coin packs and a free gift on a timer, set in a catalog asset. Real money goes through IPuzzlePurchaseProvider; a demo store that charges nothing is included.
  • Profile with an editable name, twelve avatars, four frames and the player's totals. The avatar picker fits any screen, landscape and portrait, and refits when it turns.
  • Leaderboard with three boards (stars, time on the current level, level reached), medals for the top three and the player's row pinned in view. Fifty weekly bots fill it; IPuzzleLeaderboardProvider plugs in a real backend.
  • Friends with daily gifts both ways, Claim all and invites. Bot friends fill it; IPuzzleFriendsProvider and IPuzzleShareProvider plug in the real thing.
  • Seven-day daily reward and a chest on every chapter banner that opens once all of the chapter's stars are earned.
  • EditMode tests for the whole meta core.

Look

  • Juicy candy art throughout in one berry palette: a violet backdrop with sunlight and bokeh, gold-bezelled windows and buttons, raspberry window frames with cream insets, a violet board and tab bar, objects with real volume and thickness, and original icons, avatars, medals and chests. Windows pop in with a small overshoot.

  • Lilita One for headings, buttons and numbers, with a cream face gradient and an outline in the colour of whatever the text sits on (Puzzle Text Outline); Fredoka SemiBold for running text.

  • Re-skinning through one theme asset: elements name the role they play and the theme gives that role a colour. Three themes included, switchable while the game runs.

  • Screens fit every shape: the landscape shop pairs its sections side by side, the Ranks and Friends lists keep to a readable width (Puzzle Max Width), the profile card hugs its column on a tall screen, the special offer lists everything it gives with icons, and the player's pinned rank never covers another row.

  • On a wide screen the win popup moves aside so the finished picture stays in view; window titles shrink to fit their ribbon; the reward window fits any number of rewards.

  • Runs at 60 frames per second on phones by default (Target Frame Rate on the manager); Unity would otherwise hold mobile at 30.

  • Documentation: a step by step guide to turning the demo into your own game, a table of where every player-facing text lives, and a code map of the flow, events and interfaces.

  • Every text the code writes on screen is now a field on its component, so all wording can be changed without code. Every public method and property carries XML docs.

Editor

  • One window for the whole package (Tools ▸ Corn4Bit ▸ Jigsaw Puzzle): setup and scene check, level authoring, restyling and support.
  • Level wizard: grid suggestion, live cut preview, automatic import fixes, batch creation from a folder of images.
  • Play any level straight from its inspector, and clear its saved progress there.
  • Works with the legacy Input Manager and the Input System package without referencing either.
  • Self-contained animation system and assembly definitions; no third-party dependencies.

Notes

  • Requires Unity 6 (6000.0) or newer.
  • Works with the Built-in, Universal and High Definition render pipelines.
  • Bundled third-party content: the fonts Lilita One and Fredoka SemiBold, both under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, with their licences alongside them in Art/Fonts/. Everything else (sound effects, UI art, demo pictures) was made for this package, and none of it depicts a real product, brand or trademarked design.
